## Rule 01-21 — the hard rule this plan must honour

**Never soften an assertion into one its mechanism's absence could satisfy, and never delete a mechanism to
make a test pass.** For task 2 specifically: each new source gate must be **seen failing with its subject
violated** before it is kept. A gate that passes whether or not the identifier is present is not a gate — it
is decoration that will read green forever. The two ways that happens here are both real and both guarded:

1. **A walk that finds nothing.** If the recursive read of `web/src` returns an empty list, every "appears
   nowhere" assertion passes vacuously. The gate therefore asserts the walk found three specific files in
   three different subdirectories before it asserts anything about their contents.
2. **A stripper that eats the file.** The COST-03 gate must strip comments (see below), and a stripper that
   returns an empty string makes the gate pass vacuously. The gate therefore asserts the stripped source
   still contains `export function UsagePage` before it asserts the absence of anything.

Both guards are permanent assertions in the file, not a one-time check.

## Why the COST-03 gate must strip comments first

`web/src/pages/Usage.tsx` lines 18–19 are a module doc comment that **names the very identifiers the gate
forbids**, on purpose: *"There is no `toFixed`, no `Intl.NumberFormat` and no `$` put together here, on
purpose: a second formatter in the browser is how two screens start disagreeing about one lesson."* A naive
`includes()` over the raw source would go red on the correct file — a gate that is wrong on day one and gets
softened or deleted on day two. Strip block comments and whole-line `//` comments first.

A trailing `// …` comment on a code line survives the strip and would turn the gate red. That is the
**fail-safe direction** and is left as-is: red-because-a-comment is a two-minute conversation; green-because-
a-comment is the failure this gate exists to prevent.

The PROV-13 gate does **not** strip comments: its four identifiers appear nowhere under `web/src` today, not
even in prose, so raw matching is both stricter and simpler. The consequence, which task 2 states: do not
write a comment under `web/src` naming them.

## Why the `$` half is not in the formatter gate

The COST-03 prohibition has three halves. Two are already tested on the payload: `$0.00` never reaches it
(`usage.test.ts:607`), `under $0.01` rather than a rounded-away zero (`:623`), and `weakestSource` decides a
mixed lesson's label (`:587`). The third — "no number may be formatted anywhere in the browser" — is the one
with no gate, and the gate `04-05-PLAN.md` declared is specifically "no numeric formatter appears in the
page". A raw `$` grep over `Usage.tsx` would match the six `${…}` template interpolations at lines 68, 74, 99
and 109 and be useless. The gate asserts the three formatter identifiers; the `$`-assembly half stays where
it is already covered.
